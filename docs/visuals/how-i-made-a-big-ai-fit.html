01 / What the AI is doing

It builds an answer.
One little piece at a time.

An AI model is a huge collection of numbers learned during training. Those numbers help it use what you’ve written to choose the next piece of its answer. Then it does that again. And again.

03 / The operation I built

Keep the favorites close.
Bring in the rest on demand.

I built software that organizes the model across three places, keeps frequently used pieces nearby, and moves missing pieces into place when the AI needs them.

The real engineering behind the analogy
  • The model: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, delivered in Local Inference Lab’s lossless FP4-CSF format. Project measurements used jcartu’s abliterated derivative.
  • The hardware: two 96 GB RTX PRO 6000 GPUs, about 124 GiB of usable host RAM, and two NVMe drives. These are powerful professional cards; the challenge is running a model much larger than their combined memory.
  • The contribution: the custom CSF expert-streaming engine, prepared split expert packs, GPU and host-memory caches, placement learned from traffic, and coordinated data movement.
  • The distinction: expert streaming doesn’t discard experts or further reduce their numerical precision. It changes where prepared weights are kept and when they are moved. The original model and its existing compressed format came from other teams.
  • More speed: the existing DSpark mechanism proposes several text pieces for the main model to verify together. That works alongside the streaming system.
  • Measured range: code 94–102 tokens/sec; prose 69–78; thinking mode 84–88. The headline is a favorable workload, not a promise for every conversation.
  • Sources: this project’s README, measurement log, and technical design. The 495.6 GB checkpoint size and expert architecture also appear in the model card supplied for this explanation.
